research

The title of my doctoral project is ‘Why missions fail: Rethinking European statecraft in the age of net-zero innovation policy’. It aims to expose the institutional roots of policy failure beneath the decarbonisation strategies adopted by major European governments since the Paris Agreement. The goal is to set the foundations for a more purposeful approach.

Leading arguments suggest that decarbonisation strategies fail because of the limited capabilities of public administration (neo-developmental theory), government capture (public choice theory), or regulatory gridlock (abundance theory). I claim that these theories are at once only partially true and vastly insufficient as a compass to chart a path forward. To find one, I argue that we must reappraise how governments work and what it takes to transform them.

In times of paradigmatic policy change, public managers play a critical role in translating grand political mandates into everyday administrative action. Yet, their discretion is heavily informed by pre-extant structures, processes, and tools of government. In the lack of a vision for long-term state transformation, the outcome of this dialectic is ‘mission-washing’: ambitious political rhetoric without substantive institutional change.

Thus far, European decarbonisation has been held back by a technocratic bias for policy solutionism that stifled organisational learning in the public sector; has severed state bureaucracies from market and societal dynamics; and effectively prevented the democratic anchorage of the green transition. To go beyond ‘mission-washing’ will require the pursuit of a new political and technical agenda centred upon long-term state transformation. For the lack of better expression, we may tentatively call this the ‘experimentalist statecraft’ agenda.

To explain why missions fail and sow the seeds of this long-term agenda, the project builds upon +200 elite interviews and +180 documents gathered in 6 months of fieldwork across four countries. Data has been analysed with a multi-method research design which includes a QCA of 32 European decarbonisation strategies, and a comparative process tracing analysis of 4 most different European countries (NL, FR, UK, DK).

The thesis revolves around 10 chapters and 5 papers – two of which are now published, in Research Policy and Policy Design & Practice. You can find them in the publications section of this site. I am now entering the academic job market for postdoc positions.
